🩺 Athlete Diagnosis
A 46-year-old, 196 cm / 84 kg Pro-division athlete with a strength profile that is finished and an endurance-integration profile that is unfinished. Every directly-trained quality moved fast and hard: Sled Push 204→245 kg in eight weeks, WB clean line 30→50 in three Saturdays, VO₂max +5 points in nine weeks. Every quality left untouched stayed exactly where it was — WB above 50 under fatigue never appeared because it was never trained; running past 10 km continuous never appeared because it was never trained. The pattern cuts both ways: it built the sleds into weapons and it is why the race ended at the one station whose ceiling was never pushed.

Sydney's 1:48:24 DQ is a bad results line and a clean diagnostic. Raced four days after respiratory illness onset, on HRV-rebound clearance that measured autonomic recovery and said nothing about respiratory function. Avg HR 154, 84+ minutes in Zone 4/5 — full effort. High heart rate with collapsing pace is the signature of impaired oxygen delivery, not motivation loss. The Wall Ball DQ happened at exactly the station whose 50→100 gap was never closed, under fatigue deeper than anything ever simulated.

The structural failure: Phase 3 of the Sydney build carried 100% of the WB ceiling work in one 3-week block, and illness consumed all three weeks. A plan that concentrates its most important unresolved capacity into one late, short, unrepeatable window is fragile by design. December corrects this structurally — the highest-risk capacity gets the longest runway and the most redundancy.

Two behavioral patterns matter for how this plan is written. You overshoot prescribed intensity when fresh (probe entries at 4:41/km against a 4:55–5:10 plan, fragment runs at 4:33 against 5:00–5:15) — overshooting entries burns the fatigue budget the session needed elsewhere. You execute discipline when the rule is explicit and binary (the W21 "stop at clean failure" lesson applied perfectly at W25; the W16 HRV-27 cancellation was correct and self-initiated). You respond to hard rules; you drift under vague guidance. This plan uses hard numeric caps everywhere.

📈 Progression Rules
  1. Run volume: +10%/week max; deload weeks −40%. Long run +10min per step, held two weeks before the next.
  2. Run pace: a tier advances only after two clean sessions at the current tier (pace held, HR under cap, no calf signal).
  3. WB ceiling: +5 reps per successful probe. Missed probe → same target next slot. Two consecutive misses with green health → programming change, never a bigger jump.
  4. WB density (Phase 3): progression is fewer/shorter breaks at 100 total, never more than 100.
  5. Sled: progression is rest reduction only. Load frozen ≤204kg until after Melbourne.
  6. Compromised pace: −5 sec/km per two-week block, only when current pace holds across all rounds without drift >10s.
  7. Strength: maintenance doses (2×/wk); loads 80–85% of Brisbane-era numbers; zero strength PR attempts this build.
  8. Everything: technique > number, always. A clean 65 outranks an ugly 72. The rep before the first ugly rep is the result.

🛡 Recovery Decision System — 4 Amendments
The Sydney-build G/Y/R system carries forward intact — it worked; every correct cancellation in the record came from it. Four amendments, each purchased with Sydney evidence.
Amendment 1 — Sleeping HR Joins HRV
W22–W26 proved HRV runs chronically suppressed (21–48ms) while performance stays adequate; HRV alone over-blocks and, worse, its rebound falsely cleared Sydney. New gate pair: sleeping HRV trend and sleeping HR vs. ~53–56 baseline. Sleeping HR ≥+4 = Yellow regardless of HRV; ≥+7 = Red.
Amendment 2 — Illness Rules, Binary
Below-neck symptom (cough, chest, wheeze) → zero training until 72h fully clear, then 3-day graded return. Above-neck only → Z1–Z2 permitted, zero quality/probes. Fever → 7 days min before quality work. Race go/no-go: any respiratory illness onset within 7 days of race day caps the race at training pace or withdraws it — decided on paper before race week, never by feel. This is the direct price of Sydney.
Amendment 3 — B12-Cycle Scheduling
Injections ~end of month (next ~Jul 29). 5 days before each is a known calf-risk window: long runs shorten, Saturday probes downgrade to build sessions. Calendar: ~Jul 29, Aug 29, Sep 29, Oct 29, Nov 27 (pulled forward so race day sits ~2 weeks post-injection — the demonstrated best window).
Amendment 4 — Cut Rules, Consolidated
Drop one tier if: HR over cap rep 2–3; calf >3/10; pace drift >15s/km; WB set >1:08 fragments; BBJ R2 >2:10 (30m). Stop entirely if: calf 4/10; sharp pain; cardiac sensation; two consecutive technical failures; subjective "wrong." Hierarchy unchanged: cardiac → cramp/acute calf → sleep → HRV+sleeping-HR → calves → body feel → in-session data → athlete intent last.
