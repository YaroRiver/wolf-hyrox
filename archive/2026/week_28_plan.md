## Why This Week Is Structured This Way

Endurance science has a well-documented "open window" effect: infection risk rises sharply for roughly 24–72 hours after a hard endurance effort, because the same effort that provided fitness stimulus also transiently suppresses immune function. This athlete raced hard (avg HR 154, max 172) four days after first reporting respiratory/sinus symptoms. Layering training load onto that combination is how a manageable post-race fatigue turns into a second illness episode or a lingering one. There is no season, no probe, and no capacity target riding on this week. There is only the cost of getting the next decision wrong.

Secondary reason: a Wall Balls disqualification under heavy fatigue is inferred to involve a technical/form breakdown (§5 of the comparison document). Shoulders, forearms, and the chronic calf/Achilles zone all took repetitive load under a compromised movement pattern in the final station, on top of 8 km of increasingly labored running. Even without a specific injury report, this is not a body to load again within days.

## Hard Gates Before ANY Run / WB / Sled / Jump Work Resumes

All of the following must be true, not just some:
1. No respiratory symptoms (cough, chest sensation, blocked/active sinus, voice change) for 72 consecutive hours
2. No fever at any point in that window
3. Sleeping HRV trending back toward the W27 pre-race range (41–66 ms sleeping was observed Jun 29–Jul 1) — a single good morning is not a trend
4. Sleeping HR back at or below the athlete's normal ~53–56 bpm baseline
5. No calf, Achilles, under-knee, or shoulder pain at rest or on light movement
6. Sleep ≥ 7h00 for at least 2 of the prior 3 nights

If any gate fails, the answer is another rest day, not a modified session. This is not a Yellow/Red calibration week — it is binary: cleared or not yet.
