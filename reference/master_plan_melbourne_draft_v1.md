## 1. DIAGNOSIS (corrected 2026-07-05)

**One sentence:** a strength-finished, overload-responsive 47-year-old whose Wall Ball capacity is fully proven at race distance, whose Sydney race ended on a navigational error under extreme fatigue, and whose running needs rebuilding specifically for the compromised, fatigued state HYROX demands — not from zero, but from a dormant, older running base.

**The Sydney DQ — confirmed by athlete testimony and cross-checked against both race screenshots (2026-07-05).** Wall Balls were completed in full: 100 reps, judges praised the execution. The race app shows the DQ recorded against "Wall Balls" only because that is the station where the ruling landed for scoring — it is not evidence of a WB failure. The actual cause: on Run 8, the final run before the Wall Balls station, the athlete ran the wrong (inner) loop. The pre-race briefing had explicitly stated that an incorrect loop is an automatic disqualification, no warning. **This reverses the prior working hypothesis** ("WB technical breakdown under fatigue") carried in `analytics/race_comparison_melbourne_vs_sydney_2026.md` and the original `athlete_profile.md`. Both have been corrected in place (2026-07-05).

**The same failure mode already showed up once before, at lower cost.** Melbourne 2025 (13 Dec, 1:33:44, finished, ranked #603/#29) shows two large anomalous splits — Run 6 (14:19, rank 746) and Run 7 (21:18, rank 745) — confirmed by direct screenshot review to be **lap-count penalties** (missed laps, time added — roughly 11 minutes across the two legs), not a stoppage or GPS artifact. Read together with Sydney: **navigation/lap-counting under fatigue is a confirmed, twice-occurring failure pattern** — penalized at Melbourne, disqualifying at Sydney. This is the cheapest, most decisive fix available for December.

**Wall Ball capacity is proven — read carefully what that does and doesn't mean.** 100 reps clean under full 8-station race fatigue happened once, at Sydney, under conditions no training session can fully replicate (real running fatigue stacked from 7 prior stations, a judge, race noise, no do-over). That is genuinely stronger evidence than any training probe — but it is n=1, it was not fast, and it has never been repeated under controlled conditions where rate and technique can actually be measured and trained. The correct read: **capacity is proven, speed and repeatability are not yet solved.** This plan's WB work (§8) accordingly still trains full-fatigue exposure every week from Phase 3, not just isolated speed drills — the job is to prove the 100 again, faster, on demand, not to assume it and only chase a clock.

**Running is the real physical rebuild, but not from a novice base.** The athlete carries a genuine distance-running history (periods of ~80 km/week, 15 km × 5 days) — this predates the Brisbane/Sydney builds by a long, unconfirmed margin and is not current, retained fitness. The current tracked baseline (Feb–Jul 2026: ~15–20 km/week typical, longest continuous run logged 10.03 km) is what this plan programs from. The old background is a soft signal for faster-than-novice re-acquisition of running economy — not a basis for skipping the rebuild.

**The central behavioral trait — the organizing principle of this plan, stated plainly, not hedged.** Every capacity this athlete overloaded above prescription became a weapon: sled push 204→245 kg, sled pull to 204 kg working, farmers carry run at 300 m with 64 kg (32+32 kg) unbroken against a prescription that called for shorter, lighter, rest-broken sets, static grip holds at 45 kg per hand for 60–90 s beyond anything programmed, Wall Ball line pushed to 150 total reps in a session. **Every one of his best results came from him overloading past what was written.** This plan does not talk him down from that — it is written to give that instinct explicit lanes on the stations where the tissue cost of going hard is genuinely low (sled, carry, grip, WB volume when fresh), and to reserve caution only where the physiology actually requires it (running volume ramp rate on a rebuilding aerobic/tendon base, WB density/rate work under true full fatigue repeated weekly for months). This is a judgment about tissue mechanics, not about his age — dosing running volume conservatively for anyone reactivating a dormant running base after a multi-month layoff is standard training science regardless of age, and this athlete's own data shows he responds fastest and best when the overload lane is explicit and the guardrail is a real signal (form breakdown, actual pain), not an arbitrary number.

## 4. THE OVERLOAD METHOD (organizing principle)

**Principle:** on stations where tissue cost of pushing past prescription is low (sled, carry, grip, WB volume fresh), the athlete overloads past the written number — that is the demonstrated, proven mechanism of every PR in this build. The plan states the axis and the real guardrail (form breakdown, technical standard, actual pain signal), not an arbitrary cap.

| Station | Overload lane (athlete-driven, encouraged) | Where the plan still holds a line |
|---|---|---|
| Sled push/pull | Load and rest-reduction, whenever fresh and moving well | Frozen at ≤245 kg push / ≤204 kg pull working — already exceeds race requirement by >20%, no further chase needed |
| Farmers carry | Distance and unbroken duration past prescription (proven: 64 kg × 300 m unbroken) | None — this station is a weapon, keep training it as one |
| Grip | Static holds past programmed duration, heavier than programmed | Stop at grip failure, not before |
| Wall Ball (fresh days) | Volume (150+ total), 10 kg ball | Full-fatigue 100-rep reps are for rate/density/repeatability work, not for chasing higher volume — capacity is proven, but only once; these sessions prove it again |
| Running | Pace within the week's prescribed tier, once earned | Weekly volume ramp rate (see §7) — this is the one lane where going faster than the plan actually raises injury risk on a rebuilding base, regardless of how it's tolerated week to week |

**Result:** the athlete keeps the exact mechanism that produced every one of his best results, on every station where it is safe to do so. The one station where the plan pushes back is running volume progression — not because of age, but because tendon/aerobic adaptation to a large volume increase lags the athlete's own perceived readiness by weeks, and that lag is where overuse injury actually happens, independent of how strong or willing the athlete feels in the moment.

## 8. WALL BALL SPEED & REPEATABILITY SYSTEM — target 5:00 (5:30 floor / 4:45 stretch)

Capacity is proven — once, at Sydney, under conditions no gym session fully replicates. It has not been repeated, and it has not been fast. This system trains rate, density, **and** full-fatigue repeatability together — the goal is to make the 100 boring and reliable, not just possible.

- **Fresh volume days (Phase 1):** 10 kg ball, 125–150 total reps, technique + rate — the athlete's own overload lane, unrestricted (§4).
- **Density progression:** cut rest 60→45→30 s at 100 total, hold ≤2.5 s/rep.
- **Fatigue-depth progression:** partial (3–4 station) fatigue in Phase 2 → full 8-station fatigue weekly from Phase 3.
- **Rate trajectory to race:** SIM-1 ≤5:45 → SIM-2 ≤5:15 → SIM-3 ≤5:00 → race 5:00 (A) / 5:30 (B).
- **Standard defense:** monthly video vs judging standard — not because the standard was ever missed (it wasn't; the race proved it), but because rate work under fatigue is exactly where form drifts first.

## 10. NAVIGATION-UNDER-FATIGUE WORKSTREAM (new, confirmed-priority)

The cheapest, most decisive fix available — caused the Sydney DQ outright and the Melbourne penalties.

1. **Lap-count in every sim and every couplet run with a loop** — count aloud, log it, rehearse counting while depleted so the pattern survives race fatigue.
2. **Fixed watch-lap / course-marker protocol** — one method, decided by SIM-1, unchanged after.
3. **Course walk in race week** — memorize loop structure, inner/outer lane rules, turns, laps per run, before race day, not during it.
4. **Explicit rehearsal of the specific Sydney failure mode** — a fatigued run into a station where the course briefing specified an outer-only lane. Build this exact scenario into at least one sim.

## 11. RECOVERY & ILLNESS SYSTEM

**Illness protocol (binary):** below-neck symptom → zero training until 72 h clear, then 3-day graded return, no HRV appeals; above-neck only → Z1–Z2, zero quality/probes; fever → 7 days minimum. **Race go/no-go:** respiratory onset within 7 days of race → training-pace or withdraw, decided on paper before race week, not on race-day feel.
