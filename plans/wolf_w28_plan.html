⚡ Why This Week Is Structured This Way — The Open Window
Hard endurance efforts transiently suppress immune function for roughly 24–72 hours afterward — the "open window" effect. This athlete raced hard (avg HR 154, max 172) four days after first reporting respiratory/sinus symptoms, then flew home the same day. Layering any training load onto that combination is how a manageable post-race fatigue turns into a second illness or a lingering one. A Wall Ball disqualification under heavy fatigue also implies a technical/form breakdown — shoulders, forearms, and the chronic calf/Achilles zone all took repetitive load under a compromised pattern in the final station, on top of 8 km of increasingly labored running. This is not a body to reload within days.
⛔ Hard Gates — ALL Must Be True Before Any Run / WB / Sled / Jump Work Resumes
1. No respiratory symptoms for 72 consecutive hours
2. No fever at any point in that window
3. Sleeping HRV trending back toward the 41–66 ms pre-race range — a trend, not one good morning
4. Sleeping HR back at/below normal ~53–56 bpm baseline
5. No calf, Achilles, under-knee, or shoulder pain at rest or light movement
6. Sleep ≥ 7h00 for at least 2 of the prior 3 nights
If any gate fails: another rest day, not a modified session. This is binary — cleared or not yet — not a Yellow/Red calibration.
